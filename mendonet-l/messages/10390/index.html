154 pregnant women given wrong Down's test results

Source: Electronic Telegraph (UK)
By Paul Stokes

MORE than 150 pregnant women were wrongly informed that they were at low
risk of giving birth to children with Down's syndrome because of a computer
error which went undetected for four months.

The women, who were having pre-natal checks at nine hospitals in South
Yorkshire, Lincolnshire and Nottinghamshire, had their details analysed at
the Northern General Hospital, Sheffield. Health officials have contacted
154 mothers-to-be since the mistake was discovered, to offer them clinical
support and advice.

The women, now 18 to 35 weeks pregnant, were given computer checks using a
system called Pathlan which calculates the risk of a baby being born with
Down's syndrome. Personal details, including age, weight and the length of
pregnancy, along with results from blood samples are fed into the computer
for analysis.

The women had originally been given reassuring results after the routine
screening tests, which are offered to all expectant mothers when they are
between 14 and 18 weeks pregnant. They have now been told that there is a
higher risk factor and had the correct information been known at the time
they would have offered further tests into the possibility of them having a
Down's syndrome baby.

Under the Abortion Law, severely handicapped babies can be aborted at any
time during the pregnancy, but in normal circumstances 24 weeks is the
maximum.

One of the women Debra Royston, 32, of Firth Park, Sheffield, who is 23
weeks pregnant, said: "This is like a nightmare, no mother should have to go
through this. The doctors I have spoken to say there is a one in 150 chance,
but that is no comfort at all." The initial result she received showed that
she had a one in 570 chance of the baby being affected, but the figure was
amended when the software error came to light. She now has to decide whether
to go ahead with an amniocentesis test, a process which can endanger unborn
children if carried out late in pregnancy.

Ms Royston, who has a two year old son Connor, said: "I want to have the
tests done because I want to know if there is something wrong with the baby,
but then I daren't have them done because they could kill my child. I cannot
believe believe that something like this can happen.

I was told the errors went back to January so some women involved will have
no choice, they will be about to give birth if they haven't already. My fear
is that if my baby is healthy and I have these further tests it could cause
a miscarriage because I have a weak placenta."

The women involved were patients at Barnsley District General, Chesterfield
Royal, Doncaster Royal Infirmary, Grimsby Maternity Hospital, Sheffield's
Jessop Hospital for Women , Rotherham District General, Scunthorpe General,
Bassetlaw Hospital Worksop and the Northern General.

The Pathlan system, not in use in any other hospital in Britain for the same
purpose, was found to have been providing inaccurate readings at the
Northern General's Department of Immunology from Jan 4 to May 24 this year.
It is understood that the fault was caused by the software wrongly
interpreting patients' ages as part of the calculations.

All the women involved are being informed of the revised test results and
given the opportunity to discuss them with their midwife. They are also
being offered clinical support, advice and further diagnostic tests where
appropriate. The Northern General said no patients who had previously
undergone amniocentesis tests were affected.

A full inquiry into how the errors occurred has been launched and will be
led by Dr Lindsey Davies, regional director of public health. Dr Colin
Hardisty, the medical director at the Northern General, said: "This is a
most unfortunate incident. It only affects a small number of those people
who were tested. On behalf of the trust I would like to sincerely apologise
to all the women and their families for whom this will have caused anxiety
and concern.

"Our prime concern is to ensure these women are now seen speedily. Although
the women directly affected are being contacted personally, anyone with any
concerns or who would like further information should call NHS Direct on
0845 4647."

Rape and Incest Victims Reject and Regret Abortions

Source: Elliot Institute

Springfield, IL -- A woman becomes pregnant during a rape. Elsewhere, a
12-year-old incest victim is six months pregnant. Is abortion in such cases
necessary or even helpful?

Not according to a new survey of 192 women who became pregnant through
sexual assault and either had abortions or carried term. Instead, the
consensus opinion of these women who have actually been in this situation is
that abortion in their circumstances was injurious. Indeed, the results of
this new study suggest that most women who become pregnant through sexual
assault do not even want abortions.

The results of this study are included in Victims and Victors: Speaking Out
About Their Pregnancies, Abortions, and Children Resulting from Sexual
Assault, edited by Dr. David C. Reardon, Julie Makimaa and Amy Sobie. In
addition to reporting on this survey of sexual assault victims, the new book
also includes the powerful personal testimonies of twenty women and helpful
background information on this complex issue. "Many of the women in our
sample aborted only because they were pressured to do so, and most reported
that the abortion only increased their experience of grief and trauma," said
Reardon. "In contrast, none of the women who carried to term said they
wished they had not given birth or that they had chosen abortion instead.
Many of these women said that their children had bought peace and healing to
their lives." Most women who aborted stated that abortion increased the
sense of guilt and shame they felt after sexual assault. They often reported
problems such as depression, substance abuse, broken and abusive
relationships, and suicide attempts after their abortions. "Abortion
increases the woman's sense of isolation and shame by allowing others to
pretend the problem doesn't exist," Reardon said. "By getting rid of the
pregnancy, which is a reminder of the sexual assault, it allows other people
to ignore the woman's need for understanding and honest exploration and
resolution of what she has been through." A major point raised in the book
is an appeal to allow women who have become pregnant through sexual assault
to testify at congressional hearings. "We feel that legislative hearings
would provide a forum for women who have experienced sexual assault
pregnancies to share their insights and dispel misconceptions," Reardon
said. "These women deserve the right to speak for themselves." Victims and
Victors challenges readers to develop a more caring and compassionate
attitude for women who are experiencing rape or incest pregnancies. It asks:
how can we best meet the needs of these women and their children? What
should their loved ones, doctors, pastors and counselors be doing to lend
their support in this time of crisis? How should lawmakers react when faced
with legislation regarding abortion in cases of sexual assault? "For too
long, pro-lifers have been backed into a corner on this issue," Reardon
explained. "Population controllers have exploited people's compassion for
rape and incest victims to weaken abortion laws and gain acceptance for
abortion on demand. It's time to give these women a chance to speak out for
themselves and let the truth be known."

Victims and Victors: Speaking Out About their Pregnancies, Abortions and
Children Conceived in Sexual Assault, Edited by David C. Reardon, Julie
Makimaa, and Amy Sobie / Available from Acorn Books, June 2000.
